Context

A system that worked at an earlier stage, and stopped scaling.

The product manages the full collection journey — invoicing and payment reminders, debtor communication, collection workflows, analytics, and invoice-level detail — for clients across several industries.

The redesign was part of a broader move away from a legacy system that had become difficult to use in everyday operational work. It supported the business well enough early on, but its flows were inefficient, its interactions fragmented, and its structure increasingly hard to extend.

My role was to shape a more coherent and trustworthy product experience — not only to raise the visual quality of the interface, but to support what operational teams actually do in a high-complexity financial environment, and to give managers something they can make decisions from.

Invoice overview

The screen operators live in all day.

The invoice overview is the centre of the product. Everything an operator needs to judge a case — what is owed, what has been sent, what came back — has to be readable at a glance.

Before

A two-part layout: brief invoice information with the management actions on the left, and an event log on the right showing everything that happened to the invoice — when it was sent, through which distribution channel, and its status and stage at each point.

After

More information, arranged so it reads as less. Alongside the event log and history, the screen now carries the finances behind the invoice — total against remaining amount. Every management action moved under a single Manage invoice menu, and operators can compose a new message or reply to a debtor's email directly from the event log.

What changed, and why

  • One place for actions — a single menu instead of controls scattered across the page, so nothing is discovered by accident.
  • Money made visible — remaining amount sits next to the total, because that is the number the conversation is actually about.
  • Reply where the thread is — answering a debtor happens inside the event log, so the message stays tied to its invoice.

Analytics

Numbers you can act on, not just look at.

The analytics page makes complex collections data easier to read, compare and act on.

KPIs are grouped into clear visual sections — DSO trends, receivables aging, payment matching performance — so the page gives a high-level overview first and enough detail underneath to monitor operational health over time. The aim was a calmer reporting experience that supports quick interpretation instead of overwhelming the reader.

Calls

Supervising live conversations without losing context.

The Calls page helps operators monitor active conversations, manage queues, and step in when they are needed.

An overview of live calls sits next to queue visibility and an AI-assisted support widget, so a supervisor can see volume and detail at the same time, respond faster, and handle busy periods with more confidence. The goal was a real-time workspace where human operators and AI agents work side by side rather than in separate tools.

Payments

Matching a payment that doesn't match anything.

Not every incoming payment lines up with an invoice. The manual matching flow lets operators resolve those cases in a clear, controlled sequence — with full visibility before anything is confirmed.

  1. Find the unmatched payments

    A grid lists every incoming payment that hasn't been linked to an invoice yet.

  2. Assign it manually

    A dedicated matching screen lets the operator link the payment to the right invoice.

  3. Split across items

    The amount can be divided across different items within a single invoice; anything left over can be ignored or refunded.

  4. Confirm the allocation

    A summary shows exactly how the payment was split, so the operator confirms with full sight of the result.

Communication

Everything the customer was told, in one thread.

Operators manage customer correspondence inside the system, without switching to external tools.

Emails, paper letters, attachments and reply actions live in one structured workspace, which makes it easier to review outstanding cases, respond consistently, and keep every message tied to the invoice it belongs to.

Closing

What the redesign is really for.

Collections work is high-stakes and detail-heavy: an operator is looking at someone's money, and a manager is deciding what to do next from the same screen. Most of the design work was deciding what could be pushed one level down without being lost — so the surface stays quiet and the detail is still one click away when it matters.
